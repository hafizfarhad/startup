# Sell the Comparison, Not the Table

No one has shipped the founder's exact combination, but the half of it that is easiest to build has already been proven not to be a business. **No merchant of record, Stripe Tax, or compliance vendor offers a neutral, no-login, per-state-sourced and dated nexus checker**: every vendor "free tool" is gated behind an account plus a connected Shopify/Stripe feed or CSV upload (TaxJar, Numeral, Kintsugi, TaxCloud), a lead-capture form (Avalara), a downloadable spreadsheet (Anrok) or a 30-minute call (Zamp), and the merchants of record publish stale 2019-2024 blog posts with no table and no calculator because their pitch is that thresholds do not matter. But **two independents (nexusbystate.com and taxesledger.com) built essentially the proposed US product in 2026, reached page one for "economic nexus threshold by state", and sit at Similarweb global ranks of 6-8 million with no printed visit count and no paid product**. At the same time Kintsugi and Numeral (which closed a $100 million Series C on 23 September 2026) give away live nexus monitoring on the user's own sales data for free, Stripe Tax bundles threshold monitoring at no charge, and AI Overviews cut position-1 click-through roughly in half on the informational queries a threshold table ranks for. The founder's genuine differentiators are three things the market lacks entirely: a neutral, numerically specific "merchant of record vs Stripe + a named compliance tool" break-even comparison built from real vendor prices; a row-sourced, effective-dated table of non-resident digital-services VAT thresholds (every free table either conflates domestic with non-resident thresholds or omits the 2025-26 regimes in Brazil, Malawi, Botswana and the Philippines); and a versioned, API- or MCP-delivered dataset with change alerts for developers and agent builders. The verdict: the US threshold table plus wizard is a feature vendors already give away and should be treated as citation bait; the comparison engine and the international dataset are the product; and the realistic ceiling is a bootstrapped publisher earning roughly **$3k-15k a month at 50k visits**, with acquisition by a vendor as the likeliest exit, not a venture-scale company.

## No vendor ships the wizard, and the two indies who did have no traffic

The research surveyed three vendor categories plus the independent long tail, and the pattern is consistent: vendors publish threshold tables as SEO assets and gate every interactive tool behind data ingestion. TaxJar's Sales & Transactions Checker is "at no charge" but "Starting a free trial of TaxJar's service is required," and it works by connecting Amazon, PayPal or Shopify or uploading a CSV of current- and prior-year transactions ([TaxJar Support](https://support.taxjar.com/article/562-how-much-does-the-sales-transactions-checker-cost)). Avalara's Free Sales Tax Risk Assessment asks the user to tick states and self-attest whether sales exceed thresholds, collects business details Avalara "may use ... to contact you about Avalara products and services," and shows no threshold sources or dates ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/learn/nexus/nexus-risk-assessment.html)). Kintsugi's free Nexus Study and Numeral's free Monitoring tier both require connecting stores or billing platforms ([Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/nexus-study); [Numeral Pricing](https://www.numeral.com/pricing)); Anrok's free resource is a spreadsheet template whose own page concedes "a simple spreadsheet may not be able to keep track of the ins and outs" ([Anrok](https://www.anrok.com/resources/complimentary-nexus-template-to-assess-exposure)); Zamp's free plan is a human-delivered one-time nexus analysis with a "30-minute session with a senior sales tax professional" ([Zamp Pricing](https://zamp.com/pricing)).

The merchants of record are further behind, by design. Paddle's US sales-tax explainer was published 7 March 2019 and its SaaS taxability guide states "These are the laws as of 2020"; both end by recommending Paddle ([Paddle Blog](https://www.paddle.com/blog/us-sales-tax-changes-what-software-businesses-should-know); [Paddle Resources](https://www.paddle.com/resources/saas-sales-tax)). Lemon Squeezy's January 2024 article gives two state examples and cites Quaderno, a tax-software vendor, rather than any state Department of Revenue ([Lemon Squeezy Blog](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/blog/saas-sales-tax-usa)); Polar's tax doc offers a single Texas $500,000 example and no state list ([Polar Docs](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/tax)). None of the seven MoRs surveyed publishes the list of US states in which it is itself registered, and the only MoR-built interactive tax tool found, Paddle's "SaaS Sales Tax Agony Index," now redirects to a blog post ([tax-agony.paddle.com](https://tax-agony.paddle.com/)). The MoR commercial incentive is to argue thresholds are irrelevant, so this gap will not be filled from inside the category.

Stripe sits in between. Its docs contain 52 per-region pages with thresholds, included-transaction notes and a link to each state's tax authority, plus a public grace-period table, but no page carries a last-updated date, and there is no single downloadable table, no public calculator and no API that exposes thresholds ([Stripe Docs: United States](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/united-states); [Stripe Docs: Use Stripe to register](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/use-stripe-to-register)). Its marketing guide disclaims that Stripe "does not warrant or guarantee the accurateness, completeness, adequacy, or currency of the information" ([Stripe Resources](https://stripe.com/resources/more/sales-tax-nexus-laws)), and its registration doc carries an explicit instruction, apparently for its agent integration, to "Guide, don't advise: don't tell the user which jurisdictions they must register in" ([Stripe Docs: Registering](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/registering)). That sentence is the clearest statement in the research of why no vendor will build the founder's wizard: the liability of a determination is something every vendor routes to "your tax advisor."

## Vendors now give away the computation, not just the table

The strategic fact the founder must absorb is that the free vendor offer has moved past the threshold table to live monitoring on the user's own data, which a static site cannot replicate. Stripe Tax "tracks your Stripe-processed sales (minus refunds) based on each customer's location and compares these sales against local tax registration thresholds," shows a "Needs attention" status and emails the account owner "within 1 or 2 days" of a crossing; threshold monitoring is listed as "Included" in both Tax Basic and Tax Complete, and Stripe Tax fees accrue only "where you're registered to collect taxes," so a seller who has registered nowhere runs monitoring at zero marginal cost ([Stripe Docs: Monitoring](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/monitoring); [Stripe Tax pricing](https://stripe.com/tax/pricing)). CSV imports of third-party transactions are free during public preview, extending monitoring to non-Stripe channels for anyone willing to map every line to a Stripe tax code ([Stripe Docs: Imports](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/imports)). Shopify Tax goes a step further with a "Monitoring" status at 80% of a threshold, free on the first $100,000 of sales ([Shopify Help Center](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/taxes/us/us-tax-liability); [Shopify Tax](https://www.shopify.com/tax)).

The compliance vendors have gone furthest. Kintsugi's $0 plan includes "Free global exposure monitoring (physical and economic nexus)," "Nexus threshold alerts," "AI-powered product categorization" and CSV imports, charging only from $75 per filing or registration ([Kintsugi Pricing](https://trykintsugi.com/pricing)); Numeral's Monitoring tier is free, with "$75 per filing" and "$150 per registration" on the paid plan ([Numeral Pricing](https://www.numeral.com/pricing)). These are not hobby budgets: **Numeral raised a $100 million Series C led by Insight Partners on 23 September 2026, bringing its total to $157.5 million** ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/numeral-raises-100-million-for-sales-tax-solution/)), and Anrok closed a $55 million Series C in October 2025 at a $525 million valuation ([Anrok](https://www.anrok.com/resources/announcing-series-c)). The threshold table is now the top of a free funnel for at least four funded vendors whose unit economics work because monitoring converts into $75-150 filings and registrations.

The limits of those tools are real but narrow. Stripe monitors "only Stripe-processed sales or imported transactions," assumes "all sales are taxable at the destination," cannot "differentiate between marketplace and non-marketplace sales," excludes the seller's home state, and does not notify until a business has had $10,000 in prior-year revenue ([Stripe Docs: Monitoring](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/monitoring)). Competitors make a living on the multi-channel gap, with Galvix noting "a seller whose combined sales across all channels cross a state's $100,000 threshold may never receive a Stripe alert" ([Galvix](https://www.galvix.com/article/stripe-sales-tax-automation-guide/)). A static wizard with manual entry does solve multi-channel aggregation for someone who already knows their per-state totals, and it serves the sub-$10,000 seller Stripe ignores, but that is a thin slice of a thin market, and Kintsugi's free CSV import already addresses it.

One underexploited angle does come from the MoR side. Lemon Squeezy's docs are the only official text to scope the relief precisely, saying "you shouldn't need to report sales tax for sales you make through Lemon Squeezy" ([Lemon Squeezy Docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/payments/sales-tax-vat)); it follows that a seller who also sells through their own Stripe checkout, app stores or invoices still has to track nexus on those channels, and no MoR page spells this out. A wizard that asks "what share of your revenue runs through the MoR?" and shows residual exposure poses a question no MoR will ask its own customers.

## The real differentiators are provenance, cross-category pricing and the non-resident column

Staleness in vendor tables is systemic and documentable, which is the best evidence for the founder's verification-levelled dataset. TaxJar's flagship table, bylined 12 September 2026, still shows Louisiana at "$100,000/year in gross revenue, or ... more than 200 separate transactions" and South Dakota at "$100,000 ... or more than 200 transactions" ([TaxJar](https://www.taxjar.com/sales-tax/economic-nexus)), although Louisiana repealed its transaction test effective 1 August 2023 ([Avalara, June 2023](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2023/06/louisiana-drops-economic-nexus-transaction-threshold.html)) and South Dakota's SB 30 did the same from 1 July 2023 ([SD DOR](https://dor.sd.gov/businesses/taxes/sales-use-tax/2023-legislative-updates/)). Numeral's handbook, published 7 July 2026, lists Kentucky at "$100,000 in sales or 200 transactions" with no mention of the 1 August 2026 repeal enacted by HB 757 in April ([Numeral](https://www.numeral.com/blog/economic-nexus); [Avalara, April 2026](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2026/04/kentucky-removes-transaction-threshold-taxes-data-brokering-services.html)). The neutral institutional chart from the Streamlined Sales Tax Governing Board is headed "as of January 11, 2023" ([SSTGB](https://www.streamlinedsalestax.org/for-businesses/remote-seller-faqs/remote-seller-state-guidance)), Wikipedia's thresholds are "as of July 2025" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_nexus_in_the_United_States)), and miniwebtool's "Updated: June 7, 2026" checker admits its thresholds are "as of 2024-2025" ([miniwebtool](https://miniwebtool.com/sales-tax-nexus-checker/)). The failure mode is not wrong dollar amounts but lagging removal of transaction tests across the 2023-2026 wave, and a page-level date cannot signal which rows were re-checked; **per-record `source_url` plus `last_verified`, as API Ninjas, Cbox and Kintsugi's sources section do, is the differentiator** ([API Ninjas](https://api-ninjas.com/api/salestax); [Cbox README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cboxdk/us-tax-dataset/main/README.md); [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus)). The honest caveat is that Kintsugi already publishes DOR sources and verification dates per state, and the universe of corrections is 46 jurisdictions with a handful of changes a year, so provenance is necessary for credibility but is not by itself a moat.

The pricing comparison is a stronger claim because no neutral version exists. The most complete price index, salestaxpricingindex.org, states "This site is operated by TaxCloud, Inc., a sales tax compliance provider included in these comparisons," grades every figure A through G by source confidence, and covers eight compliance tools but no merchants of record ([Sales Tax Pricing Index](https://salestaxpricingindex.org/avalara-pricing)). Zamp's "Zamp vs Anrok vs Numeral" discloses no dollar figures for anyone ([Zamp](https://zamp.com/blog/zamp-vs-anrok-vs-numeral)); the independent aggregator costbench lists Anrok at "From $499/user/mo" against Anrok's own "$100 per market per month" ([CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/tax-software/avalara/); [Anrok Pricing](https://www.anrok.com/pricing)). The only sourced cross-category comparisons are two independent blog posts from the past fortnight. aliteq.com (3 October 2026) models a $29 plan at $1.34 per US sale for Stripe plus Billing plus Stripe Tax, $1.95 for Paddle, $2.10 for Lemon Squeezy and $2.36 for Stripe Managed Payments, and derives a break-even formula under which, at $300 a month of filing cost, the MoR pays off below roughly $27,500 MRR for Paddle, $12,800 for Lemon Squeezy and $9,200 for Managed Payments ([aliteq.com](https://aliteq.com/merchant-of-record-vs-stripe-tax)). flaviocopes.com (29 September 2026) prices $10,000 a month of $50 sales at $350 on Stripe, $470 on Creem, $480 on Polar Pro or Dodo, $600 on Paddle or Lemon Squeezy and $1,450 on Gumroad ([flaviocopes.com](https://flaviocopes.com/payment-provider-fees/)). Neither models a named compliance tool's actual price ladder; aliteq uses generic filing-cost bands. **No published comparison anywhere runs a seller's own numbers through Numeral's $75 per filing and $150 per registration, Kintsugi's $75, Avalara's $403 per registration, Shopify's $75 per return or Stripe's $90-1,500 Tax Complete tiers alongside MoR take rates** ([Numeral Pricing](https://www.numeral.com/pricing); [Avalara Pricing](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/products/pricing.html); [Shopify Help Center](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/taxes/shopify-tax/automated-filing/pricing); [Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/understanding-stripe-tax-pricing)). That is exactly what a 16-vendor comparison wired to a wizard can do, and the MoR fee schedules make it necessary: Lemon Squeezy adds 1.5% for international cards, 1.5% for PayPal and 0.5% for subscriptions ([Lemon Squeezy Docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/fees)); Polar's May 2026 restructuring runs from 5% + 50¢ on the free tier to 3.4% + 30¢ at $400 a month, plus $2 per month per active payout ([Polar Docs](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/fees)); Dodo charges 4% + 40¢ domestic, +1.5% international, +3% PayPal and $30 per dispute ([Dodo Pricing](https://dodopayments.com/pricing)). A seller's true cost depends on their mix, and only a calculator can hold the mix.

The international extension is the one component with no incumbent at all. There are at least seven free vendor country tables, yet none cites a tax-authority source per row, only Anrok and Dodo separate resident from non-resident thresholds, and none offers JSON or an API ([Anrok VAT index](https://www.anrok.com/vat-software-digital-services); [Dodo thresholds](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/vat-gst-registration-thresholds)). Spot checks against 2025-26 ground truth show the problem is structural. Avalara's VATlive table gives Nigeria "NGN 25 million," the domestic threshold, where the FIRS non-resident regime uses USD 25,000 ([Avalara VATlive](https://www.avalara.com/vatlive/en/global-vat-gst-on-e-services.html); [KPMG Nigeria](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ng/pdf/tax/guidelines-on-simplified-vat-compliance-regime-for-non-resident-suppliers.pdf)); it gives Indonesia the domestic IDR 4.8 billion threshold instead of the IDR 600 million or 12,000-user PMSE rule ([Commenda Indonesia](https://www.commenda.io/indonesia/vat-registration-for-foreign-companies)); and it still shows Kenya's KES 5 million threshold, which the KRA removed effective 1 July 2023 ([KRA FAQ](https://www.kra.go.ke/helping-tax-payers/faqs/vat-on-digital-marketplace-supply)). Quaderno's table, updated April 2026, carries Nigeria at "NGN 25,000," a unit error ([Quaderno](https://quaderno.io/blog/digital-taxes-around-world-know-new-tax-rules/)). Malawi's 17.5% regime from 1 April 2026, Botswana's 14% regime with a BWP 500,000 threshold from 1 June 2026, and Brazil's zero-threshold registration requirement from 1 August 2026 are absent from most tables and from Stripe Tax's 104 supported locations ([Fonoa Malawi](https://www.fonoa.com/resources/blog/malawi-vat-foreign-digital-services); [vatcalc Botswana](https://www.vatcalc.com/botswana/botswana-vat-on-non-resident-digital-services/); [KPMG Brazil](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/05/brazil-indirect-tax-reform-nonresident-sellers-digital-platforms.html); [Stripe supported countries](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries)). With the OECD counting more than 116 jurisdictions that have adopted its digital-trade VAT standards ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2026/09/governments-reforming-tax-systems-to-boost-growth-but-revenue-pressures-continue-to-mount.html)), the field that decides registration for a non-US SaaS seller, the non-resident threshold with its legal instrument, effective date and B2B treatment, is the field every free table gets wrong or omits.

## The arithmetic caps a 50k-visit site at roughly $3k-15k a month

Demand in the US niche is modest. Similarweb estimates the vendors' own domains at roughly 24,000-33,000 visits a month (trykintsugi.com about 24k, numeral.com about 27k, taxjar.com about 33k) ([Similarweb: Kintsugi](https://www.similarweb.com/website/trykintsugi.com/); [Similarweb: Numeral](https://www.similarweb.com/website/numeral.com/); [Similarweb: TaxJar](https://www.similarweb.com/website/taxjar.com/)), which implies total organic demand across all players in the low hundreds of thousands of visits a month, not millions. The head query "economic nexus threshold by state" is contestable: three independents reached the top ten of the proxy SERP while Avalara, TaxJar and the Sales Tax Institute did not, but vendors still hold positions 1, 2 and 4 ([Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus); [nexusbystate.com](https://nexusbystate.com/economic-nexus)). "Do I need to collect sales tax in Texas" and "sales tax software pricing" returned zero independents, only vendors, accounting software and the Texas Comptroller ([Xero](https://www.xero.com/us/guides/texas-sales-tax/); [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/sales-tax/)). The exception is the merchant-of-record cluster: "Paddle vs Lemon Squeezy" and "Stripe Tax vs Paddle" are dominated by independents and indie blogs because neither Paddle nor Lemon Squeezy nor Stripe publishes head-to-head pages ([cut-the-saas.com](https://cut-the-saas.com/guides/stripe-vs-paddle); [Fungies](https://fungies.io/paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy/)).

AI Overviews compress whatever share an independent wins. Advanced Web Ranking's July 2026 snapshot puts desktop position-1 CTR at **20.02% on all SERPs but 9.69% when an AI Overview appears, a 52% drop**, with commercial-intent queries falling from 11.91% to 3.51% ([Advanced Web Ranking](https://www.advancedwebranking.com/seo/organic-ctr)); Seer Interactive measured organic CTR falling from 1.76% to 0.61% on AI Overview queries but found sites cited inside the Overview saw 35% higher CTR ([Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/news/seer-interactive-research-featured-in-inc.-analysis-of-ctr-and-ai-overviews)). "What is the nexus threshold in Texas" is the archetypal query an Overview answers from vendor or Wikipedia pages, so the realistic upside of a provenance-first page is being the cited source, not winning the click.

Monetisation is thin and skewed. Quaderno pays affiliates "30% commission each month for every paying customer you refer" ([Quaderno Support](https://support.quaderno.io/article/544-quaderno-affiliate-program)); a third-party listing puts Paddle at 15% recurring for 12 months on platform fees ([CommissionDex](https://commissiondex.com/programs/paddle/)); Creem pays a flat $15 per activated merchant ([Creem](https://www.creem.io/creemers)); Dodo caps payouts at $1,000 or one year ([Dodo](https://dodopayments.com/partner-program)); Kintsugi, Numeral and Anrok run partner programmes built for accountants and agencies and publish no rates ([Kintsugi Partners](https://trykintsugi.com/partners); [Numeral Partner Program](https://www.numeral.com/partner-program)). Against that, Rewardful's 2026 benchmark across 2,847 programmes finds "The average referral-to-sale conversion rate is 0.8%" and "98.72% of affiliates never generate a sale" ([Rewardful](https://www.rewardful.com/articles/state-of-saas-affiliate-programs-report)). Modelling 50,000 visits a month with an assumed 2-5% outbound click rate yields 8-20 new paying customers a month and a steady-state affiliate range of roughly $1.5k-15k, mid case $3-6k, after a six-to-twelve-month ramp, discounted further because users referred to Kintsugi or Numeral may stay on free tiers indefinitely. Sponsorship has one concrete anchor, getdeploying.com's $2,500 a month on 10,000 visitors with 116 cloud providers competing for attention ([Niall Doherty blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25115821-his-side-project-2500-month-after-7-months?tab=author)); with perhaps 15-20 plausible sponsors in sales tax, two to four slots at $750-2,000 is a more defensible $1.5k-8k. For the raw dataset, willingness to pay should be assumed near zero because every vendor and every state publishes thresholds free; the VAT-rate API precedent, vatlayer at $9.99-99.99 and Vatstack at $15-150 a month, shows paid products survive by selling delivery, validation and SLAs rather than numbers ([vatlayer](https://vatlayer.com/pricing); [Vatstack](https://vatstack.com/pricing)), so 20-50 subscribers at $10-100 adds $500-3,000. **Combined, 50,000 visits a month plausibly earns $3k-15k, mid case $5-8k, and the lower half if most traffic is informational rather than commercial.** No keyword-volume data could be obtained, so the 50,000 figure is a target, not a forecast; the two existing independents show that page-one presence does not guarantee even 5,000.

## To be a business it must sell what vendors structurally cannot publish

The neutral sites that beat vendor content share traits nexus thresholds lack. PCPartPicker, affiliate-funded since 2011, draws roughly 1.57 million visits a month with 12.89 pages per visit and a 6:37 dwell time because it sells compatibility checking and price alerts across retailers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCPartPicker); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/pcpartpicker.com/)); Levels.fyi bootstrapped to a $2 million run rate by 2021 on salary data that employers withhold ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=28190195)); tld-list compares 3,484 extensions across 54 registrars with prices refreshed every six hours and draws about 187,000 visits a month ([tld-list.com](https://tld-list.com/); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/tld-list.com/)); esimdb aggregates over 400,000 plans from 220 providers via API feeds at about 141,000 visits a month ([esimdb](https://esimdb.com/about); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/esimdb.com/)). Each sells a cross-vendor comparison no single vendor can publish credibly, builds a freshness moat on automation, and offers tooling that brings people back. US nexus thresholds offer none of this: 46 jurisdictions, a few changes a year, published free by every vendor and every state. The closest analog, ec2instances.info, was acquired by Vantage in 2021 and "mainly used ... as part of their marketing efforts," after which a community fork notes maintenance lapsed ([Vantage](https://www.vantage.sh/blog/vantage-has-acquired-ec2instances-info); [GitHub fork](https://github.com/shield0225/cloud-instances.info)); that is both the likeliest exit for a neutral nexus site and its failure mode.

Applied to the founder's project, four shifts turn a feature into a product. First, lead with the comparison engine, not the table: a seller-mix break-even calculator that takes US versus international share, subscription share, PayPal share, payout country and the number of states crossed, then prices Stripe plus Stripe Tax, Stripe Managed Payments, each MoR with its surcharges, and each compliance tool at its published per-filing and per-registration rates. This is the one cluster where independents already rank by default, where aliteq proved a dated, sourced piece can compete with Dodo, Fungies and Creem content, and where no one has used tool-specific pricing. It should also surface the residual-nexus question the MoRs avoid.

Second, give the US table away under a permissive licence and sell its delivery. The market has already signalled the shape: API Ninjas sells a nexus endpoint returning `statute_cite`, `source_url` and `last_verified` on $39-199 a month plans ([API Ninjas](https://api-ninjas.com/api/salestax); [API Ninjas pricing](https://api-ninjas.com/pricing)); TaxLocus ships an MCP server and REST API with "every value backed by a citation" ([TaxLocus](https://taxlocus.com/)); Cbox's dataset carries a 120-day re-verification alarm but a licence that forbids redistribution ([Cbox README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cboxdk/us-tax-dataset/main/README.md)). A CC-BY table with per-row provenance is the asset most likely to be cited inside AI Overviews and by agent builders, and a versioned API with change alerts, taxability matrices and filing frequencies is the thing 20-50 developers will pay $10-100 a month for. The developer-tier bundling of thresholds with citations and MCP connectors signals that the near-term buyer is an agent or LLM builder, not a tax department.

Third, make the international dataset the flagship rather than the extension. It is the only component without any incumbent, the errors in existing tables are structural (domestic versus non-resident conflation, missing effective dates, no B2B or platform-rule columns), and the audience is the long tail no vendor serves: the EU reports only 5,628 non-Union OSS registrants at end-2024 against aggregator estimates of tens of thousands of non-US SaaS companies ([EU Commission](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/news-reports/report-application-vat-e-commerce-package-2024_en); [Ascendix](https://ascendixtech.com/number-saas-companies-statistics/)), Stripe does not alert below $10,000 of prior-year revenue, and Quaderno's $24 and Lovat's €35 entry plans only make sense once revenue exists ([Quaderno pricing](https://quaderno.io/pricing/); [Lovat pricing](https://vatcompliance.co/prices/vat/)). Each row needs the legal instrument, the effective date, the non-resident threshold distinct from the domestic one, the B2B treatment (Philippines withholding, South Africa's 2025 reverse charge), deemed-supplier rules and user-count triggers; that schema is itself the product.

Fourth, fund it from the vendors who benefit from neutrality. Numeral's and Anrok's rounds mean sponsorship budget exists, and the comparison-and-calculator positioning makes the site valuable to vendors as a referral source precisely because it is not theirs. The site should expect to lose the "how do I comply in Texas" cluster to vendors and state DORs permanently and should not spend on it.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question from "has anyone built this?" to "why has no one who built it made money?" The answer is an asymmetry: vendors can afford to give away the US threshold table, and now the monitoring on top of it, because each is an onboarding funnel into $75-150 filings; what they cannot give away is a credible cross-vendor price comparison that includes their competitors and the merchant-of-record alternative, or a registration determination they would be liable for. TaxCloud's price index is the exception that proves it, disclosing its conflict in the first paragraph. The founder's specification as written is weighted toward the part of the market that is free and shrinking under AI Overviews, and only lightly toward the parts that are empty.

The decision-ready version: build the US dataset as open, provenance-first citation bait and never as the product; put the engineering into the seller-mix break-even calculator and the non-resident VAT schema; sell delivery (API, MCP, change alerts) and sponsorship, not data; and plan for a ceiling in the low five figures a month unless the dataset becomes infrastructure that agent builders depend on. If the goal is a venture-scale company, this is the wrong wedge. If the goal is a durable bootstrapped publisher with a plausible acquisition by a vendor that wants the neutrality it cannot manufacture, the difference is a business, but only after the build order is inverted.
