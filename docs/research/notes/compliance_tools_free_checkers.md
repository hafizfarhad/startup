# Free Nexus Checkers, Public Threshold Tables, and Threshold/Rate Data Products (US sales tax) — state of the market as of 2026-10-07

All pages below were fetched or searched on 2026-10-07 unless otherwise noted. "Vendor" = a company selling compliance software; "indie" = small sites with no disclosed operator. Where a page summary could not be verified verbatim, that is flagged.

## KQ1 — Which vendors offer a FREE "enter your sales by state, see where you have nexus" tool without a product subscription? Signup? Sources/dates? Thresholds only or also taxability?

### Takeaway
No major vendor offers a truly anonymous, manual-entry, sourced-and-dated nexus checker. Every vendor "free" tool is either (a) gated behind an account/free-trial and requires connecting a billing platform or uploading transactions (TaxJar, Numeral, Kintsugi, TaxCloud, Zamp), (b) a lead-capture questionnaire with no visible threshold sources (Avalara, Source Advisors), or (c) a downloadable spreadsheet (Anrok). The only no-login manual/upload checkers found are small indie sites (sales.tax "Snapshot", miniwebtool, nexusbystate.com), and of those only nexusbystate.com shows per-state sources and a "sources checked" date; miniwebtool explicitly runs on 2024–2025 thresholds. None of the free tools combine thresholds with taxability except Zamp's human-delivered free plan.

### Inferences
- The vendor "free nexus study" pattern (Numeral, Kintsugi, TaxCloud, TaxJar) is a data-ingestion funnel: value is unlocked only after connecting Shopify/Stripe/QuickBooks or uploading a CSV, which doubles as onboarding. None exposes the threshold table it uses, its sources, or verification dates to the user.
- Avalara's and Source Advisors' tools are lead-capture forms; the "assessment" is effectively a lookup of self-selected states against an unseen table.
- A manual-entry, no-login, per-state-sourced checker with visible verification dates is a genuine gap among vendors; only indie sites attempt it, and the best-sourced one (nexusbystate.com) has no named operator and no data export.

## KQ2 — Which public threshold tables exist, who maintains them, how often updated, do they cite state sources, do they expose data (JSON/API/download)? Is there any neutral (non-vendor) dataset with provenance?

### Takeaway
Dozens of HTML tables exist but almost none expose machine-readable data. The best-maintained vendor tables (Sales Tax Institute "As of 8/1/2026"; Kintsugi "Last updated Sept 26, 2026" with per-state DOR sources and verification dates; TaxJar Sept 12, 2026 with state links) are HTML-only. The only neutral institutional table (Streamlined Sales Tax Governing Board) is headed "as of January 11, 2023" and is partially stale. Two small open datasets with per-record provenance appeared in mid-2026 (Cbox us-tax-dataset, PolyForm Internal Use; TaxLocus taxlocus-data, CC-BY-4.0) but both are tiny (1–3 GitHub stars) and vendor-adjacent. No government open-data series exists.

### Inferences
- Threshold tables are a marketing-SEO commodity: Kintsugi, TaxJar, Avalara, Numeral, TaxCloud, Zamp, Sphere, Commenda, Galvix and a long tail of indie domains (nexusregister.com, sails.tax, salestaxstate.com, taxesledger.com, eightx.co, mysalestaxfirm.com) all publish 2026 tables. Sourcing quality ranges from per-state DOR citations with verification dates (Kintsugi, nexusbystate.com) to none (Numeral handbook, Avalara guide, taxmap.ai).
- The only institutional neutral table (SSTGB) is the most authoritative in links but has a 2023 header and uneven row updates; Tax Foundation's table is a 2019 research artifact, not a maintained dataset.
- Machine-readable, provenance-bearing threshold data exists only in two mid-2026 GitHub projects (both commercially motivated, both near-zero adoption) and one commercial API (API Ninjas). A neutral, redistributable (e.g., CC0/CC-BY), per-record-sourced threshold dataset with explicit verification dates does not yet exist.

## KQ4 — Evidence of errors or staleness in vendor and public tables that a provenance-first dataset would address

### Takeaway
Staleness is systemic and documentable: as of October 2026 TaxJar's flagship table (dated Sept 12, 2026) still shows 200-transaction tests for Louisiana and South Dakota (both repealed in 2023); Numeral's July 2026 handbook omits Kentucky's Aug 1, 2026 repeal enacted in April; taxmap.ai shows Illinois and Kentucky with 200 transactions; miniwebtool runs on "2024–2025" thresholds; the SSTGB chart is headed "as of January 11, 2023"; Tax Foundation and Sovos pages date to 2019–2023. Even Avalara's own list of 17 states that dropped transaction tests gives no dates or citations. Discrepancies in how the same law is cited (Anrok "HB 2755" vs Illinois DOR "Public Act 104-0006") show why per-record source URLs matter.

### Cited Findings
- TaxJar table byline "September 12, 2026" still states Louisiana "$100,000/year in gross revenue, or makes sales into Louisiana in more than 200 separate transactions" and South Dakota "Sales of $100,000 in South Dakota, or more than 200 transactions" — [TaxJar](https://www.taxjar.com/sales-tax/economic-nexus); contradicted by Louisiana's Aug 1, 2023 repeal — [Avalara, June 2023](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2023/06/louisiana-drops-economic-nexus-transaction-threshold.html) and [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/louisiana-removes-200-transaction-threshold-from-economic-nexus-rules); and South Dakota SB 30 effective July 1, 2023 — [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2023/South-Dakota-drops-Wayfair-transaction-threshold.html), [SD DOR](https://dor.sd.gov/businesses/taxes/sales-use-tax/2023-legislative-updates/)
- Numeral handbook "Published: July 7, 2026" lists Kentucky "$100,000 in sales or 200 transactions" with no note of the Aug 1, 2026 removal — [Numeral](https://www.numeral.com/blog/economic-nexus); Kentucky HB 757 had passed April 14, 2026 — [Avalara, April 2026](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2026/04/kentucky-removes-transaction-threshold-taxes-data-brokering-services.html)

### Inferences
- The failure mode is not usually wrong dollar amounts but lagging removal of transaction tests (2023–2026 wave: SD, LA, ND, IN, WY, NC, AK, UT, IL, KY). Tables without per-row verification dates cannot signal which rows were re-checked after each repeal.
- Even dated pages (TaxJar Sept 12, 2026; miniwebtool June 7, 2026) can carry rows that were never re-verified, so a page-level date is insufficient; per-record `last_verified` plus `source_url` (as API Ninjas, Cbox, and Kintsugi's sources section do) is the differentiator.

### Gaps
- No systematic audit of all 46 threshold rows across vendors was performed; staleness evidence is based on spot checks of IL, KY, LA, SD (plus AK, IN, NC, WY for TaxJar).
- Could not confirm the current state of Sovos's threshold table or Zamp's/Sphere's state tables.
