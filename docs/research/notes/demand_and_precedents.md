# Demand evidence and precedents: can a neutral, provenance-first US sales-tax data/comparison site win against vendor content? (as of 2026-10-07)

Method note for the writer: "Who ranks" below comes from a web-search tool's top-10 result lists fetched on 2026-10-07, not from a logged-out Google SERP, so treat position order as a proxy and treat AI Overview presence as unobserved. All Similarweb/Semrush numbers are third-party estimates from their free public pages; Similarweb's free page does not print a visit count for very small sites, so "rank only" means the site is below its reporting floor (roughly a few thousand visits/month). Ahrefs/Semrush keyword-volume pages and Google Trends are JavaScript-only and could not be fetched; no keyword volume figures were obtainable (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The head data query is contestable: three independents with no brand authority reached the top 10 of this proxy SERP in 2026, while Avalara/TaxJar/Sales Tax Institute did not. Vendor pages still take positions 1-2 and 4, so the independents' reachable share is the lower half of page one.
- The "how do I comply in [state]" and "software pricing" queries are vendor-locked in this sample; an independent would be competing with 7+ funded vendor content teams plus state governments and Gartner's Software Advice.
- MoR "vs" queries are the only cluster where independents already win, and they win by default: the MoR vendors do not publish neutral head-to-heads. Compliance vendors (Anrok, Numeral, Zamp, Sphere, TaxCloud) do publish "vs" pages about each other, so that cluster is vendor-locked.
- If Similarweb's ~25-35k/month estimates for numeral.com, trykintsugi.com and taxjar.com are even roughly right, a 50k-visit/month independent would out-traffic the vendors' own domains, which suggests the whole niche's organic demand is in the low hundreds of thousands of visits/month across all players, not millions.

### Inferences
- Common success factors across the winners: (1) the comparison itself is impossible for any single vendor to publish credibly (registrar prices, broker fees, eSIM plans, component prices); (2) a freshness/coverage moat built on automation (6-hour price refresh; 400k plans via provider APIs); (3) tooling that creates return visits (PCPartPicker's 12.89 pages/visit and 6:37 dwell vs 1.6-2.2 pages for the nexus sites); (4) data the vendors actively withhold (salaries). Nexus thresholds have none of these: ~46 jurisdictions, changes a few times a year, published free by every vendor and by the states.
- The closest analog to a nexus dataset is VAT rates, where the free authority table exists and the paid products survive at $10-150/month by selling API uptime, validation (VIES) and SLAs, not the numbers. A nexus/taxability dataset would likely price in that band, not in Exploding Topics' $1-4k band, which is justified by proprietary trend signals.
- Vendor acquisition of a neutral data site for marketing (ec2instances.info -> Vantage) is a realistic exit shape in this niche given vendors' funding (Q5), and it is also the failure mode: post-acquisition maintenance lapsed.
- Where vendors' own content wins is exactly where the question is "how do I comply" rather than "which one is cheaper"; a neutral site should expect to lose the compliance-how-to cluster to vendors and state DORs.

### Gaps

- No sourced explanation of why tld-list or esimdb outrank registrars/eSIM vendors (no SEO case study found); the inference rests on their coverage/freshness claims and traffic estimates.
- Levels.fyi's own current revenue is undisclosed; only third-party estimates.

## Key Question 4: Monetization realism — affiliate terms, conversion rates, sponsorship and dataset pricing; plausible revenue at 50k visits/month

### Inferences
- Modelled affiliate revenue at 50k visits/month (assumptions labelled): if 2-5% of visits click out to a vendor (no sourced benchmark for comparison-site outbound CTR; this is an assumption), that is 1,000-2,500 referred visitors; at Rewardful's 0.8% that yields 8-20 new paying customers/month. Per-customer value: Quaderno 30% x $49-149 = $14.70-$44.70/month for up to 24 months ($176-$1,073 lifetime if retained); a Numeral/Kintsugi customer filing in 10 states (~$750/month) at an assumed 15-20% share for 12 months = ~$1,350-$1,800 (rate assumed from Anrok's 15%; Numeral/Kintsugi rates undisclosed); a Paddle referral at 15% of a 5% platform fee = 0.75% of referred GMV for 12 months (~$900/year on $10k MRR); Creem a flat $15; Dodo at most $1,000. Stacking 8-20 new customers/month at $150-$1,500 lifetime value gives a steady-state range of roughly $1.5k-$15k/month after a 6-12 month ramp, with a mid case around $3-6k.
- Two realism discounts apply on top: (a) Kintsugi/Numeral/Anrok run partner programs built for accountants and agencies (application, portal, referral form), not cookie-tracked publisher links, so anonymous clicks from a content site may never be credited; Kintsugi pays nothing until the referral is on a paid package, and both Kintsugi and Numeral give nexus monitoring away free (Q5), so many referred users will stay on free tiers; (b) Rewardful's 98.72%-never-convert figure means the median content affiliate earns zero; the plan must assume the site is in the top ~1% of affiliates to earn anything.
- Sponsorship: scaling getdeploying's $0.25/visit linearly would give $12.5k/month at 50k visits, but that benchmark reflects ~116 cloud vendors competing for developer attention; the sales-tax niche has perhaps 15-20 plausible sponsors (Anrok, Numeral, Kintsugi, Zamp, Sphere, Galvix, TaxCloud, Quaderno, Avalara, TaxJar, Sovos, Commenda, Avask, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Creem, Dodo, Fungies). Two to four slots at $750-$2,000/month, i.e., $1.5k-$8k/month, is a more defensible inference, and the recently funded vendors (Q5) are the ones with budget.
- Dataset/API: because every vendor and every state publishes thresholds free, willingness to pay for the raw nexus table should be assumed near zero; the VAT-API precedent suggests $10-$100/month per subscriber is achievable only for a maintained, versioned, API-delivered dataset with change alerts and broader coverage (taxability matrices, local rates, filing frequencies). 20-50 subscribers would be $500-$3,000/month.
- Combined plausible range at 50k visits/month: roughly $3k-$15k/month, mid case $5k-$8k, and the lower half of that range if most traffic is informational ("threshold in Texas") rather than commercial ("pricing", "vs").

### Gaps
- Kintsugi, Numeral and Dodo commission rates are not public; Anrok's 15% comes from a search snippet of an unfetched page.
- Lemon Squeezy's referral/affiliate terms were not researched.
- No sourced benchmark for outbound click-through from comparison/data sites to vendors; the 2-5% assumption is unsourced.
- No sponsorship rate card from any sales-tax or fintech data site; getdeploying is the only concrete data point, and its own /sponsor and /advertise pages return 404.
- Reditus's $60-$126 EPC figure likely reflects cumulative recurring earnings on small click counts; it was not used in the base case.

## Key Question 5: Counter-evidence — vendor content teams, vendor free tools, AI Overviews

### Takeaway
Vendors with $55M-$100M rounds (Anrok Oct 2025, Numeral Sept 2026) give away not just the threshold table but live nexus monitoring on the user's own sales data for free, and 2025-2026 CTR studies show informational queries lose roughly half their position-1 clicks when an AI Overview appears; together with the near-zero Similarweb footprint of the existing independents, the evidence leans toward "feature vendors already give away" unless the site sells comparison or tooling vendors cannot.

### Cited Findings
- Numeral raised a "$100 million" Series C led by Insight Partners on 2026-09-23, total raised "$157.5 million" since 2023, reporting a "327% year-over-year increase in total transaction volume" and planning to "launch accounting partner program" — [finovate.com](https://finovate.com/numeral-raises-100-million-for-sales-tax-solution/)
- Anrok raised a $55M Series C in October 2025 led by Spark Capital at a $525M valuation, total funding over $100M; customers include Anthropic, Mistral and Cursor (search snippets) — [anrok.com](https://www.anrok.com/resources/announcing-series-c); [fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2025/10/23/ai-tax-platform-anrok-bags-55m-in-series-c-funding/)

### Inferences
- The vendors' free offer has moved past the table to the computation: connecting a Shopify/Stripe account and getting "you crossed nexus in Illinois on this date" is something a static neutral site cannot replicate without integrations and user data; the table alone is now the top of a free funnel for at least four funded vendors.
- "What is the nexus threshold in Texas" is the archetypal informational query that an AI Overview answers from vendor or Wikipedia pages; AWR's data implies even a position-1 neutral page would keep roughly half its clicks, and Seer's data implies the realistic upside is being the cited source (35% CTR lift) rather than winning the click, which favors provenance-first pages but shrinks the traffic pool.
- The strongest counter-evidence is empirical rather than theoretical: two independents built essentially the proposed product in 2026, reached page one for the head query, and show no measurable traffic or paid product.
- The contrary signal is the MoR "vs" cluster (Q1), where independents win by default and where aliteq's break-even calculator shows a neutral, dated, source-cited piece can rank against Dodo/Polar/Fungies vendor content; a "merchant of record vs do-it-yourself compliance" cost comparison is the one sub-niche where the vendor-neutral angle is both scarce and commercially adjacent.

### Gaps
- Whether AI Overviews actually trigger on the eight target queries was not observed (tool limitation); the CTR figures are population-level.
- No data on how much of vendors' nexus-guide traffic converts to paid, or on vendor content-team budgets.
- No evidence found of a neutral site in this exact niche being acquired by a vendor; the ec2instances.info case is from an adjacent niche.
